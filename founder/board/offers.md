# Offers lens

Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The pain is real and the buyer has money. A SAR 1,300 to 2,100 paint protection film job is a high-consideration purchase, and the brief says customers do not know whom to trust. The pain the offer addresses (trust and choosing a installer) is the right pain.
- The dream outcome is clear and the effort is low on paper. Describe the job once, receive competing quotes, accept one, pay in the app. That cuts the buyer's search effort.
- There are built-in risk reversal elements. Held payment released on completion and an automatically issued warranty directly attack the likelihood-of-success problem, which is where most offers fail.
- Free to join for both sides removes price friction at entry.
- The partner side has real pull. 8 of 20 simulated partners would join, mostly because they want customers. A hungry supply side is a usable starting point.
- Two markets (Saudi Arabia, Egypt) and several categories give room to find a narrow beachhead.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. Perceived likelihood is near zero today. 0 of 30 simulated buyers would buy, and 27 named trust (no reviews, unknown partners, they already have a workshop and WhatsApp quotes). The value equation fails on likelihood, and the brief shows no proof: demo data only, a simulated payment gateway, no live partners with real jobs. The current alternative (a known workshop plus WhatsApp quotes) is free, fast and trusted, so the offer is competing against an incumbent that costs the buyer nothing.
2. The offer is not yet differentiated beyond convenience, and the economics leave no room to make it irresistible. The platform's own take is about 3.5% of an order, about SAR 4.65 contribution per order if it pays the gateway fee, and break-even of about 61 paid orders a day. There is no budget stated to fund a guarantee, bonuses or buyer incentives, and no stack beyond the marketplace mechanics. Partners also object to the 10% commission, the document upload and approval wait, and fear of bypass, which means the supply side can route around the platform after the first introduction and leave the platform with no revenue.
3. The market definition is too broad and the first-customer path is unproven. The brief lists paint protection film, ceramic coating, tint, home services, beauty, devices and physical products across two countries. That is several markets, not one. The brief does not show how the first 100 buyers are found or what each costs. Reaching buyers in a thin, trust-driven market with no reviews is the hard part, and the simulated panels are not real purchases.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Who exactly is the first buyer, in one category and one city, and what do they pay for today and from whom?
- Why would that buyer believe it works before there are reviews? What proof can exist within 30 days?
- What is the guarantee, and what does the platform stand behind with its own money rather than just a partner warranty?
- How quickly does a buyer receive usable quotes after posting a job, and who guarantees that number?
- What stops a buyer and partner from taking the job to WhatsApp after the first quote?
- Could the price (commission) be doubled and the platform still be the obvious choice? What does the platform provide that is worth that?
- How will the first 100 paid orders arrive, and what does each cost?
- Which one category could be dropped so the offer is about one outcome?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

- Narrow to one category in one city (for example paint protection film and ceramic coating in Riyadh) and state the offer in one sentence.
- Replace simulated evidence with real evidence: a live payment provider and a set number of real paid jobs completed with real partners, with real reviews and warranties issued, before further spend. The number is the founder's to set and should be stated up front.
- Define a platform-backed guarantee (for example refund or redo if the job fails the warranty promise) and show it is affordable on a margin of about SAR 4.65 per order, or change the take rate or fee handling so it is.
- Add an anti-bypass reason to stay on the platform (held payment, warranty only valid when paid in app, rebooking benefits) and test it with partners.
- Show a repeatable way to reach the first 100 buyers with a measured cost per order, using real data rather than simulated panels.
