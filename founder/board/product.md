# Product lens

Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The core moment is clear and worth owning: describe the job once, receive comparable quotes, pay in the app, get a warranty. This replaces the scattered WhatsApp quote chase with one thread, and the brief names real, concrete jobs with real prices (paint protection film, SAR 1,300 to 2,100).
- Trust is built into the mechanics rather than bolted on: ID and commercial registration review, payment held until completion, an automatic warranty, and an audit log. These are the right seams to own, and they match the one objection buyers actually raised.
- Craft signals exist in the parts customers do not see: right-to-left Arabic as a first-class language, data-driven market packs for currency, VAT and phone rules, role-based access, a public storefront per business.
- The founder's tooling reported the bad numbers (0 of 30 buyers, thin contribution per order) rather than hiding them. The board can work with an honest picture.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. No single thing it does brilliantly. The brief lists car film, coating, tint, home services, beauty, devices, plus physical products shipped from stores and warehouses, across two countries. The Product lens finds a marketplace for everything, which means the first five minutes for any one customer are unproven for every category. Focus means saying no, and nothing has been cut.
2. The first experience asks the buyer to trust strangers, and the product has no proof yet. 27 of 30 simulated buyers named trust, citing no reviews, unknown partners, and an existing workshop reachable by WhatsApp. Demo data and a simulated payment gateway mean the promised moment (pay, hold, split, warranty) has never actually happened for a real person. Reviews were just added and have no content.
3. The handoffs are where it breaks, and the seams are many. Quote to acceptance, payment hold to release, job done to warranty, and partner onboarding (ID and registration upload, staff review, approval wait) are all handoffs between people and systems. The partner side already shows friction (document upload, approval wait, fear of bypass), and at about SAR 4.65 contribution per order the product cannot afford the support effort to repair broken handoffs.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What is the one thing ArmorHub must do brilliantly, in one sentence, and which categories and which of the two countries would you cut to prove it?
- Describe the first five minutes for a Riyadh car owner who wants paint protection film: what do they see, whom do they hear from, and when do they feel safe enough to pay in the app?
- What does the customer see on day one when there are no reviews and few partners? What stands in for proof?
- Where does a customer and partner meet after the quote, and what stops them from moving the rest of the job to WhatsApp?
- Which handoff has failed most often in your own testing, and who owns fixing it?
- What did you cut from the physical-products and warehouse side to protect the service experience, or did you not cut it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

- Narrow to one category and one city for the first stretch (for example paint protection film in Riyadh) and defer the other categories, Egypt, and the physical-products shipping path until the one experience works.
- Run a real end-to-end job with a live payment provider, a real verified partner and a real customer, from request to warranty, and document every seam that broke.
- Show the first real reviews and warranty issued from completed jobs on the public partner page before spending to attract more buyers.
- Cut the partner onboarding wait to something a partner finishes in one sitting, or show a fast provisional path that lets a partner quote before full approval.
- State the one-sentence product and the in-app moment that keeps the job on the platform after the quote.
